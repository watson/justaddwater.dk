By default, Eclipse highlights usage of a word, if you double click it. But IntelliJ IDEA can also do that.

[{{< img src=`/wp-content/uploads/2011/08/intellij-highlight-usages-example-annotated-crop1.png` alt=`` title=`intellij-highlight-usages-example-annotated-crop` class=`aligncenter size-full wp-image-1487` width=`595` height=`323` >}}\
](/?p=1480)

It is a very convenient feature, which highlights all occurrences of the word you place the cursor in.

To turn it on, just go to "File\>Settings" window.

Check "Highlight usages of element at caret" under "IDE Settings \> Editor"

[{{< img src=`/wp-content/uploads/2011/08/intellij-highlight-usages-of-element-at-caret-crop-300x152.png` alt=`` title=`intellij-highlight-usages-of-element-at-caret-crop` class=`aligncenter size-medium wp-image-1483` width=`300` height=`152` >}}](/wp-content/uploads/2011/08/intellij-highlight-usages-of-element-at-caret-crop.png)

Frankly, I do not understand why this is not default turned on in IntelliJ.
